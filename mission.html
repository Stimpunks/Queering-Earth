    What this cabinet is for

    Thirteen aims, and where each one is already kept.

    This site has a page for what it holds, a page for how it is made, a page for everything it has got wrong, and a page for what it knows about you. What it did not have was a page saying, plainly and in one place, what we are trying to do. That absence is not neutral: a project with no stated aim is a project whose aims can drift without anybody being able to point at the drift — which is the same objection this site makes to a quotation nobody can trace, or a number nobody can re-derive.

    On this sheet

      The whole of it, in one sentence Link to this section

      Queering Earth reads the canon closely and in public — art, literature, poetry, politics, people, and history — and asks what else any of it could have been, so that the arrangements we were handed stop looking like the only ones available.

      Thirteen aims follow, in four groups. None of them is a hope. Each one names a page where it is already being practised, because a values list you cannot check against the work is the exact thing this page is trying not to be. And the last section names, for each, the observation that would show we had quietly stopped.

      What we are claiming Link to this section

      1. Queering is a verb. The word reaches us from Queer Theory by way of Nick Walker, and what we took is the parenthesis: subverting, defying, disrupting, liberating oneself from. It names something you do to a norm, not a category you belong to, and the norms it can be done to are not only the ones about sex and gender. A site that used the word as a synonym for a demographic would be using the noun and calling it the verb. Kept on Queering is a verb, where he is quoted exactly rather than paraphrased.
      2. Normal is an invention, and a recent one. Barely two centuries old, arriving with the statistician rather than the physician, and it displaced older words that meant something else entirely — wyrd meant fate, what befalls you, before it was demoted to weird and then to defect. A thing that was invented can be read as an invention, which is a different act from complaining about it. Kept on Sheet No. 4, and it is Helen Edgar’s reading before it is this site’s.
      3. Having been canonised is not the same as having been settled. Every sheet takes a work that has already been decided about and asks what else it could have been. Not to catch anybody out — the readings here are affectionate, and several are love letters. But a text that can only be read one way has stopped being a text and become a ruling. Kept on the plate, one sheet at a time.

      How we intend to be right Link to this section

      1. Interpretation is the method, and the room left open is the product. There are no hard facts here for everything, and that is the design brief rather than a lowered bar. Our sibling Star Stuff is built on evidence and risks a wrong fact; this half of the shelf is built on reading and risks a wrong attribution. What replaces the fact is not licence. It is the obligation to show the reading’s working, so that somebody can disagree with it precisely rather than vaguely. Kept on What this is, and argued in full on the colophon.
      2. If we changed the words, they are ours. The characteristic failure on a site like this is not an invented source. It is a tightened one — a definition trimmed to fit a masthead, an object quietly generalised, a paraphrase left wearing somebody’s name. So: credit the concept, quote the original exactly, or write our own line. When we carry somebody’s idea to an object they were not writing about, the sheet says whose move that is instead of letting their authority cover our argument. Kept in the attribution ledger — see the Tuck and Yang entry, where the transposition is named on the sheet in a sentence of its own.
      3. Every quotation reaches the page from a primary, or names whose transcription it is. A search of our own library is not verification: five files agreeing with each other are one witness, not five. Where the primary can be reached we read it and say which copy, because that is how an edition difference gets caught. Where it cannot, the quotation carries the citation and the name of whoever transcribed it, rather than being dropped or quietly smoothed. Kept in the ledger, each entry dated the day a person read the source.
      4. Publish the corrections, and spend the gold on the mend. Every sheet carries at its foot the date it was mounted and the number of times we have corrected it since, each clause linked to the entry that did it. The seam is marked in the most expensive colour the palette has, because kintsugi is not that the crack shows — it is that the precious material goes on the break. A register with no corrections in it is not a clean record. It is a record nobody is keeping. Kept in the accession register, and on the provenance line at the foot of every sheet.
      5. A reading is signed, and the reader is not the maker. Woolf made On Being Ill; we made the reading of it. Those are two different people, on two different rows of the label and in two different fields of the machine-readable copy, and no page here collapses them into one name at the top. Getting that wrong would tell every agent on the web that we wrote Woolf. Kept on Who reads, and who writes, and enforced by a gate that refuses the conflation.

      What we owe whoever is reading Link to this section

      1. One document, and two ways to read it. The type here leans, deviates, and refuses to sit on the line, because a page about normativity set in a tidy grid is arguing against itself. All of that lives in the stylesheet, over ordinary semantic markup, and plain view switches it off. Never an image of text. Never a decorative copy beside an accessible one — two copies of the same words drift, and the accessible one is always the copy that rots. Kept on Plain view, and on the control at the top of this page.
      2. Accessibility measured, not asserted. Contrast is held at 7:1 in both grounds and under print emulation — the house target, not the 4.5:1 floor. Every page has one landmark, real headings, an address for every section, and a printable sheet. None of that is a promise: each claim is checked by a gate that runs before anything ships, and the gates are in the repository where you can run them yourself. Kept on How it is built, and in the tools directory.
      3. Ask nothing of you. No analytics, no cookies, no accounts, no comments, no advertising, no newsletter gate, and nothing fetched from anybody else’s server unless you press something that says so first. The one field on the site never submits anywhere, and what you search for stays in your own browser. Kept on What this site knows about you, which is checked against the code every time the code grows a capability.

      How we work Link to this section

      1. In public, with the working-out left in. The pages, the drawings, the tools, the gates, the attribution ledger, the decision log and every error we have caught in our own work are one public repository at one commit. The register records not only what we mounted but what we took down again and why. Nothing here is finished in private and then presented. Kept in the repository and in the register.
      2. Free, forkable, and yes to the machines. Everything of ours is CC BY-SA 4.0; the works we read belong to the people who made them and were never ours to license. The training crawlers and the retrieval crawlers are named in robots.txt and allowed, because a refusal beside a share-alike licence would be a contradiction somebody would eventually have to resolve. The answer is yes and the ask is attribution — so the citation trail is published in the form a machine can actually reach. Kept in robots.txt, in llms.txt, and in a Markdown copy beside every page.

      What a cabinet of readings has to do with Stimpunks Link to this section

      Queering Earth is a collaboration between the Stimpunks Foundation and More Realms. Stimpunks is a 501(c)(3) with a mission of its own, published on its own site, and it is worth quoting rather than paraphrasing — the paraphrase is where a mission usually goes soft, and paraphrase-wearing-a-name is the failure this whole site is organised against.

      The middle sentence has three verbs in it, and this site is a contribution to each one. Live with dignity is what a reading does when it hands somebody a word for themselves that is not a deficit. Challenge dehumanizing systems is what Sheet No. 4 does to normal and Sheet No. 1 does to the army you desert by lying down. Design environments that honor difference as strength is the reason the type on this site plays and the reason plain view exists in the same breath.

      The third sentence is the one that surprised us. Stimpunks’ own mission says weird — and Sheet No. 4 is the history of exactly that word being demoted from wyrd, meaning fate, to a name for people who do not fit. The sheet was not written to illustrate the mission. It turned out to be about it.

      And what it has to do with More Realms Link to this section

      The other half is Helen Edgar’s, and her own landing page states the method this site borrowed before this site existed.

      Not a place of conclusions or answers is the same commitment this site makes when it says there are no hard facts here for everything, arrived at from her side rather than ours. The tagline on the masthead is hers — post-normal possibilities, written in the founding brainstorm — and the masthead credits Nick Walker beneath it because his book carries the phrase as a subtitle, one hyphen apart. That credit is not an admission of borrowing and does not claim one; it is what you do when a collision is real and a reader will eventually find it.

      The limit, said plainly Link to this section

      What this site does not do Stimpunks’ first programme is direct financial support to individuals — rent, medical bills, equipment, creator grants, living wages. This site does none of that and should not be counted as though it did. No sheet here has paid anybody’s rent or replaced anybody’s wheelchair. If anything on these pages is worth something to you, the thing that converts it into material help is the foundation, not the artifact.

      The honest claim for a cabinet of readings is narrower, and still worth making. It is what somebody can reach at two in the morning, free, without an account, in a browser, when they have just been told again that the way they are is a defect — and find a page that takes the question seriously enough to cite its sources.

      How you could tell if we stopped Link to this section

      An aim you cannot fail is not an aim. Each of these is a condition somebody could observe from outside, with no access to what we meant, which is the only sort of accountability worth publishing.

      The aim, and the observation that would falsify it
      AimWhat would show we had stopped
      Queering is a verbThe word used here as a synonym for a demographic, or as something a person is rather than something done to a norm.
      Canonised is not settledA sheet that arrives at the verdict the work already had, and adds nothing to it but agreement.
      The room left openA sheet that closes its reading — that tells you what the poem means rather than what it could mean, and leaves you nothing to disagree with.
      If we changed the wordsA quotation here that does not match its source, with the source’s name still attached. Or our extension of somebody’s idea presented as their reach rather than ours.
      Primary sourcesA citation trail that ends at a quote site, an encyclopedia, one of our own pages, or a machine’s summary.
      Publish the correctionsA stretch of the register with no corrections in it. We are not that good; a clean run means we stopped looking.
      Reader is not the makerA page whose byline or metadata names us as the author of the work being read.
      One documentAnything readable only in the decorated version, or an image with words in it that the words are not also in.
      Measured, not assertedA contrast failure shipped and left standing, a page that comes off a printer blank, or a gate quietly run without its checking flag.
      Ask nothing of youAny request to a third party that the reader did not choose, or any attempt to find out who is reading.
      In publicA count printed in our prose that nobody can re-derive from the repository.

      Most of those are not hypothetical, and the ones that have already happened here are the reason the list reads the way it does. This site’s working tagline was once a paraphrase of Nick Walker wearing his name, and it was replaced. Eve Tuck and K. Wayne Yang were carried from settler colonialism to masking, and the sheet nearly let their authority cover the move before it was made to say so in a sentence of its own. The plate said Seven sheets above eight cards for as long as the eighth had been up — a count in our prose that nobody could re-derive, which is the last row of that table, committed by us, on this site, and found only because somebody read down the page and counted.

      Every one of those is in the register with a date on it. That is the point of keeping one.

      Who wrote this, and with what Link to this section

      The reading, the editing, and the deciding are Helen Edgar’s and Ryan Boren’s; the drafting is done in sessions with an AI assistant, directed and edited by whichever of them is answerable for the sheet. A tool does not get a row on the label, because a byline is responsibility and a tool cannot carry any — but a site that keeps a ledger of everybody else’s words owes an honest account of where its own came from, so the arrangement is described rather than left to be inferred.

      The full statement, including what is never allowed to stand as a citation, is on the colophon.
