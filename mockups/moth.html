Moth mockups

Four readings of the same homage — Nick Walker’s cover, Ezra Furman’s lilac and black, the Earth where the brain was. Toggle the ground and the plain view; each is shown large and again at the size it would sit in prose.

Variant A — the plate

The book cover read into this site’s own register: a symmetrical Victorian plate, ink line over lilac wash, the globe where the brain was. Luna-moth tails do the work the swallowtail’s tails do on the cover.

Variant B — two hemispheres

The homage made literal. A brain is two hemispheres; so is the Earth. The old double-hemisphere world map puts two circles exactly where Walker’s cover puts two lobes, and the wings step back to line so the centre carries it.

Variant C — the lilac and the black

The cover’s own logic, and the song’s: a dark panel worn on the sheet, lilac line on black. This is the only version that looks the same in daylight and in the cabinet, because it brings its own ground.

Variant D — queered

Asymmetry against the norm. One side finished, one side still line and vein, set a few degrees off. It is the verb rather than the specimen — a moth mid-queering rather than one pinned.
