Moth mockups

Four readings of one homage — Walker’s cover, Furman’s lilac and black, the Earth where the brain was. Each is shown large, then at the size and place it would take at the foot of Queering is a verb. Both grounds; the plain view drops the motion and keeps the drawing.

Variant A — the plate

The cover read into this site’s own register: a symmetrical Victorian plate, ink line over a lilac wash, the globe where the brain was. Luna-moth tails do the work the swallowtail’s tails do on the cover, so it stays a moth.

In place, at the foot of the section

Walker is naming two normativities in particular. Taking the verb further out — to a painting, a canon, a century, a diagnosis, a life — is our extension, not his sentence. The credit for the verb stays with him.

The line under our masthead owes him too. Neuroqueer Heresies is subtitled Notes on the Neurodiversity Paradigm, Autistic Empowerment, and Postnormal Possibilities — one hyphen from our own.

Variant B — two hemispheres

The homage made literal. A brain is two hemispheres; so is the Earth. The old double-hemisphere world map puts two circles exactly where the cover puts two lobes, and the wings step back to bare line so the centre carries the drawing.

Variant C — the lilac and the black

The cover’s own logic, and the song’s: a dark card worn on the sheet, lilac line on black. The only version that looks the same in daylight and in the cabinet, because it brings its own ground — and the only one that does not sit on the paper.

Variant D — queered

Asymmetry against a norm. One side finished, one side still line and vein, set a few degrees off, one antenna sparse. The verb rather than the specimen — a moth mid-queering rather than one pinned.
